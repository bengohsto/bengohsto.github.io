Research

History of education · Global and transnational history · Singapore and Southeast Asia

I work on the history of sex education, history education and the liberal arts, and more broadly on education policy and the forces that shape education systems. I was trained in global history, with a grounding in Singapore and Southeast Asian studies. I also work on historiography, intellectual history and social history.

My method is global and transnational: I connect local and global forces to compare how education systems developed. My interest grew out of my work as a peer tutor, a Ministry of Education officer and a trainee teacher. I hope my research helps make education fairer for students of every ability, identity and orientation.

Projects

A Global History of Population-Centred Sex Education, 1950s–1980s

Book project, in progress

This project traces how governments in the Global South took up sex education as an answer to perceived overpopulation from the 1950s to the 1980s. I call this process the “partial appropriation” of ideas from the Global North. It draws on the archives of the IPPF, the Population Council and UNESCO, and on national archives in Singapore, the Philippines and elsewhere, with case studies including Singapore, Manila, Accra and Montevideo.

  • BA thesis, Yale-NUS College (2022)
  • “A Glocal History of Post-Independence Singapore’s First Sex Education Curriculum, 1966–1973,” History of Education Quarterly (2024). DOI

A Commonwealth of Historians: Decolonising History at the Setting of the British Empire

This project follows historians across the former British Empire as they rethought how history should be written, focusing on the Inter-University Council for Higher Education Overseas. It covers universities in Malaya, Nigeria, Tanzania, India and Ghana, develops the idea of “partial decolonisation,” and builds on Tamson Pietsch’s work on the British academic world.

  • MPhil dissertation, University of Cambridge (2023)
  • “‘Asia, Not the European in Asia, Must Be Our Theme’,” Itinerario (2026). DOI

Malaya’s Historians: Making, Teaching, and Learning History at the University of Malaya, 1950s–1970s

Book project

Expanding my MPhil dissertation, this project studies the University of Malaya’s history department as a whole, through figures such as C. N. Parkinson, K. G. Tregonning, Eunice Thio, Wang Gungwu and Wong Lin Ken. I argue that the university pioneered the study of Malayan, Southeast Asian and world history.

  • MPhil dissertation, University of Cambridge (2023)
  • Itinerario article (2026). DOI
